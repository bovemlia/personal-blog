> “This could be Heaven or this could be Hell”
>
> — Eagles, “Hotel California”

Most founders will go through a period of pivoting. If you see that period as “pivot hell” it will be. From my experience running startups, your mentality towards your company’s state can become self fulfilling. (And this is true about life more broadly.)

## Persistence Hell

There’s also such thing as “persistence hell”.

Our launch concept, smart scales that restock coffee, had clear product market fit. The kind of product market fit where you can barely keep up. Twice. But we lost it both times.

It’s extremely hard to let go of a concept that previously had product market fit. You always feel like you’re one tweak away from getting it back. We ended up persisting for *years* after we last lost product market fit.

Now that we’ve actually committed to the pivot, it’s freeing. We’re waking up and working on what we find interesting every day! It really does feel like heaven. When I see the phrase “pivot hell” I always think, man, you should try the grass on the other side.

## So what are we working on?

We’re building Agent Duel, a battle system built on top of OpenCode. Our goal is to make a leaderboard that reflects how agents perform in real work. Leaderboard: [agentduel.com/leaderboard](https://agentduel.com/leaderboard).
